# Materials and Methods

## Study site

The study was conducted in a temperate steppe located at the Duolun Restoration Ecology Research Station, Inner Mongolia, China (42°02' N, 116°17' E, 1324 m a.s.l.).
The mean annual precipitation is c. 378 mm, and the mean annual temperature is 2.1 °C.
The total monthly precipitation and mean monthly temperature in the study site from 2013 to 2015 are shown in Fig. 1a.
During the experiment duration, the mean monthly temperature during growing season (from May to September) ranged from 11.8 to 20.3 °C and more than 80% precipitation occurred during the 5 months (Fig. 1a).
The dominant vegetation consists of perennial herbs such as *Stipa krylovii*, *Agropyron cristatum* and *Cleistogenes squarrosa*.
Standing dead, recently senesced *C. squarrosa* litter (C4 grass) was collected in September 2012, air-dried, and stored in the dark at room temperature.
The initial dissolved organic carbon (DOC) concentration, *N* concentration and C/N ratio of *C. squarrosa* litter were 12.44 mg g<sup>-1</sup>, 0.64% and 65.80, respectively.
Litter was oven-dried at 35 °C and mixed to achieve homogeneity before use.

## Field Experiment Design

An *in situ* litter decomposition experiment simulating standing litter and soil surface litter was conducted in Inner Mongolia, China.
Photos of the experiment setup were shown in Fig. S1, Supporting Information.
In brief, six steel frames, 50 cm $\times$ 50 cm $\times$ 20 cm (*l* $\times$ *w* $\times$ *h*), were erected on the ground after clipping the above-ground vegetation.

Two grams dry litter was placed into 1-mm $\times$ 1.5-mm nylon mesh litterbags (10 cm $\times$ 15 cm).
Twelve litterbags were placed under each frame on May 20, 2013.
The placement of these bags was either (i) on the soil surface to simulate the decomposition of the soil surface litter (hereafter termed the 'soil surface litter' treatment, Fig. S1a), or (ii) suspended 10 cm above the ground with the support of 1-mm $\times$ 1.5-mm nylon mesh below to simulate the decomposition of standing litter (hereafter termed the 'standing litter' treatment, Fig. S1b).
To eliminate the potential confounding effects of photodegradation, we placed the litterbags horizontally on the surface of soil or on the supporting mesh, and clipped above-ground vegetation periodically during growing seasons to avoid shading.
Measurements with a UV light meter (TAINA TN-2340; Taina Co, Ltd, Taiwan, China) showed that the litterbags at the two positions received the same amount of UV radiation (290-390 nm).

Each treatment included three replications, resulting in six steel frames in total.
Temperature and RH on the surface of the standing litter and soil surface litter for each treatment were recorded every 30 min during the growing season (from 14 July to 15 September in 2014 and 11 May to 25 July in 2015) using DS 1923 iButtons (Maxim Integrated, San Jose, CA, USA).
The sensors were deployed on the surface of litterbags with sensing area facing down to avoid direct exposure to sunlight and precipitation.

One litterbag was collected from each frame at days 31, 57, 88, 117, 318, 346, 384, 409, 474, 711 and 787.
The 12th collection of litter sample was not included because the remaining litter mass was too low to measure litter chemistry.
Subsamples of the litter were oven-dried at 60 °C for 48 h after sampling and then weighed to get the moisture content.
A fraction of the oven-dried subsample was further combusted overnight in a muffle furnace at 500 °C to determine the ash content.
The rest of the fresh litter were divided into two subsamples.
One was freeze-dried for analysis of microbial community composition.
The other was stored in refrigerator for the analysis of DOC concentration and laboratory incubation experiment.

Litter DOC was extracted by shaking 200 mg of litter with 30 mL of deionized water for 24 h in the dark at room temperature.
For the 10th (day 711) and 11th (day 787) collections, the DOC was extracted using 100 mg of litter with 15 mL of deionized water because of no enough litter mass remained.
The DOC concentration of the extract was analysed with a total carbon analyser (Multi N/C 3100; Analytik, Jena, Germany).
Specific UV absorbance (SUVA<sub>254</sub>) of the DOC solution was measured by using a UV-Vis spectrometer (Cary 60 UV-Vis; Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA).
SUVA<sub>254</sub> was calculated as the absorbance at 254 nm L<sup>-1</sup> mg$\cdot$C<sup>-1</sup> m<sup>-1</sup>.
SUVA<sub>254</sub> served as an indicator of aromatic carbon content [@Kalbitz2003][@Fellman2008].
Values of litter DOC concentration and litter remaining mass were corrected for litter moisture and ash content.

A subsample of freeze-dried litter collected in summer of 2014 (day 409, the 8th collection) and 2015 (day 787, the 11th collection) was used to assess the microbial community composition under different position treatments.
The phospholipid fatty acids (PLFAs) in litter samples were extracted using the conventional methods as described in [@Wilkinson2002], with 250-mg grounded litter for the 8th collection and 80-mg for the 11th collection because of insufficient remaining litter mass.
Individual fatty acid methyl esters were identified and quantified using the MIDI Sherlock Microbial Identification System (MIDI, Newark, DE, USA) and gas chromatograph (Agilent 6850).
The fatty acids used in the analysis of microbial community composition are listed in Table S1.
The PLFA values were also corrected for litter moisture and ash content.

## Effects of Night-time RH on Microbial Activity

To assess the effect of night-time RH on microbial activity, we conducted another experiment by placing litterbags under each treatment as described above.
Each treatment also included three replications.
Litter used for the incubation was collected in fall of 2014, air-dried and stored under a cool, dark and dry condition until weighed and placed in litter bags.
After 1 week of field incubation, litter moisture content and microbial respiration rate of litter from both treatments were measured at the times 06.00, 08.00, 10.00, 12.00, 15.00, 18.00, 20.00 and 23.30 h for 6 sunny days in 10-15 August 2015.
The dry weight of litter was assumed consistent during the 6 days.
Litterbags were retrieved from each treatment and weighed immediately to determine litter moisture content.
Litter microbial respiration was then measured by placing the litterbag into 300-mL plastic boxes, which were sealed for 30 min.
We put litterbag (containing 1 g litter) each into a 300-mL plastic box, resulting in six plastic boxes in total.
Headspace gas (10 mL) was sampled to analyse the CO<sub>2</sub> concentration with an infrared gas analyser (EGM-4; PP Systems, Haverhill, MA, USA) at the beginning and the end of the 30 min of incubation.
The difference in CO<sub>2</sub> concentration between the two measurements was used to calculate microbial respiration rate.

## Laboratory Incubation

To investigate whether the standing phase will affect subsequent decomposition after the litter falls to the soil surface and whether standing litter and surface litter will differ in their contributions to SOC formation, we incubated the litter (C4 grass with $\delta^{13}\text{C}$ = -15.522) collected from the field experiment using soils (with $\delta^{13}\text{C}$ = -23.848) collected from a pure C3 grass patch at the study site.
Laboratory incubation experiments were conducted by mixing litter samples from the 2nd, 4th, 5th, 6th, 8th, 9th and 10th collections with the soil.
Specifically, a subsample of refrigerator-stored litter was weighed and the amount was equivalent to 0.4 g moisture and ash free litter.
The litter was mixed with 40 g soil for the 2nd to the 9th collection.
Because of no enough litter remained, 0.2 g litter after correcting for moisture and ash content was mixed with 20 g soil for the 10th collection.
Each treatment included three replications.
The mixtures were incubated in 150-mL plastic jars at 25 °C.
Distilled water was added to the jars every day to maintain soil moisture at 60% of water holding capacity.
Microbial respiration rates during the first 60 days of the incubation were measured as the field incubation experiment described above.
The measurements of respiration during the incubation were at days 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 9, 11, 16, 25, 43, 60.
After 192 days of incubation, the remaining litter in each jar was separated from the soil manually, cleaned, oven-dried at 60°C for 48 h and ash corrected to determine remaining litter mass.
Total C and C stable isotopes ($\delta^{13}\text{C}$, ‰) were measured for the litter and the soil of the microcosms from the 10th collection using a Delta Plus XP isotope ratio mass spectrometer (Thermo Finnigan, Bremen, Germany).

## Statistical Analysis

The average temperature and RH on the litter surface were calculated separately for the 24-h daily, daytime (from 07.00 to 19.00 h) and night-time (from 19.00 to 07.00 h).
The DS1923's humidity sensor produces readings that are higher than the actual humidity when exposed to humidity levels of about 70% RH and higher.
This shift continues to increase while the device remains at 70% RH and above, which is called saturation drift.
We calibrated the RH data that were higher than 70% using the equation provided by the manufacturer.
The detail of the calibration procedure is described in the section of Software Saturation Drift Compensation of the DS1923 user manual.

The coefficient of variation (CV) of temperature and RH was calculated based on the equation $CV = s/\bar{X}$, where $s$ is the standard deviation and $\bar{X}$ is the mean value of temperature or RH during the measurement time.
The litter decomposition rate ($k$) was calculated based on the equation $M_{t} = M_{0}e^{-kt}$, where $t$ = time (years), $M_{t}$ = litter mass at time $t$ and $M_0$ = initial litter mass.
The cumulative C loss by respiration was calculated as

$$\text{cumulative C loss} = \sum_{i=0}^{n} R_i T_i$$

where $n$ is incubation time (h), $R_i$ is the mean respiration rate (mg C h<sup>-1</sup> g<sup>-1</sup> litter or soil) and $T_i$ is the time between two successive respiration measurements (h).

For the incubation experiments, new C from litter stored in the soil and litter SOC formation efficiency were assessed.
New C stored in soil was calculated from the distinct $\delta^{13}$C values of litter inputs and soil C using the following equation:

$$\mathrm{C}_4 = \mathrm{C}_t \frac{\delta_t - \delta_3}{\delta_4-\delta_3}$$

where $\mathrm{C}_4$ is the amount of soil C derived from C4 litter, $\mathrm{C}_t$ is the final total organic soil C pool (C3 + C4), and $\delta_t, \delta_3$ and $\delta_4$ are the $\delta^{13}$C values of the final total organic soil C pool, old C3 soil and C4 litter, respectively [@Cheng1996].
Litter SOC formation efficiency was calculated as the ratio of litter-derived soil C to the total litter C loss during the 192-day laboratory incubation.

Statistical analysis of variance (ANOVA) and linear regressions were conducted using R software (2.14.2).
For litter mass remaining, DOC concentration, cumulative C loss and litter ash concentration over different sampling dates, the data were analysed using repeated measures ANOVAs.
For $k$ value, new C stored in soil, litter SOC formation efficiency, litter moisture, PLFAs and SUVA<sub>254</sub>, the data were analysed using independent samples $t$-test.
Treatment effects were considered to be statistically significant at P < 0.05.
